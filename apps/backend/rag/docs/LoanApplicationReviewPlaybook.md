# **Loan Application Review Playbook**

**Document ID:** LA-RVW-002  
 **Version:** 1.0  
 **Effective Date:** 2026-01-01  
 **Document Owner:** Retail Credit Operations  
 **Classification:** Internal Use Only

---

# **1\. Purpose**

This playbook provides step-by-step operational guidance for analysts reviewing unsecured consumer loan applications that require manual assessment. It translates underwriting policy into an actionable review workflow and defines how analysts should evaluate applicant data, supporting documents, policy triggers, and model-supported risk indicators before issuing a final recommendation.

This document is intended to promote consistency, efficiency, and auditability in manual loan review.

---

# **2\. Scope**

This playbook applies to:

* Unsecured personal loan applications routed to manual review

* Applications with borderline risk indicators

* Applications with documentation gaps or inconsistencies

* Cases involving possible exceptions or escalations

* Applications requiring deeper analyst judgment beyond automated screening

This playbook does not replace underwriting policy. Where this playbook and formal policy differ, the underwriting policy manual takes precedence.

---

# **3\. Analyst Review Objectives**

The analyst review process is intended to:

1. Confirm whether the applicant’s profile supports repayment

2. Validate that the case complies with underwriting policy

3. Identify documentation or consistency gaps

4. Determine whether the application should be approved, rejected, or escalated

5. Produce a concise, evidence-based case rationale

6. Ensure the decision can withstand audit and supervisory review

---

# **4\. Review Philosophy**

Manual review is not a second attempt to justify either approval or rejection. It is a structured process for resolving uncertainty. Analysts are expected to:

* Apply policy consistently

* Use judgment only where judgment is permitted

* Verify material facts before relying on them

* Focus on objective evidence

* Clearly distinguish between confirmed facts, unresolved concerns, and assumptions

* Escalate when decision authority or policy clarity is insufficient

Manual review must always improve the quality of the final decision, not merely delay it.

---

# **5\. Review Workflow Overview**

Each manually reviewed application must follow the steps below:

1. Intake and case triage

2. Application completeness review

3. Core applicant profile assessment

4. Affordability assessment

5. Credit quality assessment

6. Documentation consistency review

7. Risk synthesis and decision formulation

8. Exception or escalation assessment

9. Decision note preparation

10. Final disposition and audit logging

Each step is described in detail in this playbook.

---

# **6\. Step 1: Intake and Case Triage**

## **6.1 Confirm Review Trigger**

At intake, the analyst must identify why the application entered manual review. Common triggers include:

* Borderline risk score or policy threshold

* Elevated debt-to-income ratio

* Requested loan amount high relative to income

* Incomplete or inconsistent documents

* Employment verification concerns

* Model-policy tension

* Potential exception case

* Product eligibility ambiguity

The review trigger must be captured in the case note before substantive analysis begins.

## **6.2 Establish Review Priority**

Cases should be prioritized according to:

* Time sensitivity

* Product service-level agreements

* Degree of uncertainty

* Presence of pending customer follow-up

* Escalation dependency

High-complexity cases should be assigned sufficient time for full review rather than processed under speed-only expectations.

---

# **7\. Step 2: Application Completeness Review**

Before analyzing risk, the analyst must confirm that the file is complete enough for review.

## **7.1 Required Application Fields**

At minimum, the following fields must be present and interpretable:

* Annual income

* Debt-to-income ratio

* Credit score

* Requested loan amount

* Interest rate

* Employment status

* Loan purpose

* Grade/subgrade or internal risk tier

Additional fields such as marital status, education level, and gender may exist in source systems, but must not drive adverse outcomes independently.

## **7.2 Required Supporting Records**

Where required by process, the case should include:

* Identity verification record

* Income evidence

* Employment support document

* Any clarification requested from the applicant

* Prior analyst note, if previously reviewed

## **7.3 Incomplete File Handling**

If the file is materially incomplete:

* Do not finalize the decision

* Identify missing elements clearly

* Request clarification or documentation if permitted by process

* Move the case to pending review status where applicable

A file is materially incomplete when the missing information affects affordability, credit interpretation, policy eligibility, or the ability to verify key facts.

---

# **8\. Step 3: Core Applicant Profile Assessment**

The analyst should first build a concise understanding of the applicant’s profile before examining the case in detail.

## **8.1 Create a Case Snapshot**

The analyst should summarize the following:

* Declared income level

* Debt burden

* Credit quality

* Employment status

* Requested loan size

* Stated loan purpose

* Overall initial risk impression

This snapshot is not the final decision. It is a working summary used to structure the review.

## **8.2 Initial Risk Orientation**

As a first-pass orientation, the analyst should determine whether the case appears:

* Clearly strong

* Clearly weak

* Mixed or borderline

* Incomplete or inconsistent

* Potentially exceptional

This step helps determine where to focus deeper review.

---

# **9\. Step 4: Affordability Assessment**

Affordability is one of the most important elements of manual review.

## **9.1 Review Annual Income**

The analyst should assess whether income appears:

* Sufficient relative to the requested amount

* Stable and credible

* Consistent with employment status

* Supported by any available documents

Questions to consider:

* Is the income plausible for the employment profile?

* Does the requested loan appear proportionate to income?

* Would repayment likely create material financial strain?

## **9.2 Review Debt-to-Income Ratio**

Debt-to-income ratio should be interpreted using the following framework:

* Low ratio: generally favorable

* Moderate ratio: acceptable with sufficient support

* Elevated ratio: caution

* Very high ratio: strong affordability concern

The ratio must be considered together with:

* Income level

* Loan amount

* Interest rate

* Employment stability

* Other material case facts

## **9.3 Review Loan Amount Burden**

A large requested amount may be acceptable for a strong applicant, but becomes concerning when paired with:

* Moderate or low income

* Elevated debt-to-income ratio

* Higher pricing

* Weak or borderline credit profile

The analyst should assess whether the loan request is reasonable given the overall profile.

## **9.4 Affordability Review Outcome**

At the end of this step, the analyst should classify affordability as:

* Strong

* Acceptable

* Borderline

* Weak

* Unverifiable

This classification should be referenced in the final decision note.

---

# **10\. Step 5: Credit Quality Assessment**

The analyst must assess whether the applicant’s prior behavior and risk tier support approval.

## **10.1 Review Credit Score**

Credit score should be interpreted in context:

* Strong score: supports approval

* Acceptable score: neutral to positive

* Borderline score: requires contextual review

* Weak score: strong negative indicator

The analyst should avoid relying on the score alone and instead assess whether it aligns with the rest of the case.

## **10.2 Review Grade/Subgrade or Risk Tier**

Where available, risk grade or subgrade provides a summarized risk signal. Analysts should:

* Confirm whether the grade is generally approvable

* Determine whether the grade is aligned with other case features

* Note whether the grade requires review or rejection under policy

* Assess whether compensating strengths are present if the grade is weak but not prohibited

## **10.3 Identify Mixed Credit Signals**

Mixed credit signals may include:

* Acceptable score with weak affordability

* Weak score with strong income and stable employment

* Borderline grade with otherwise strong profile

* Strong score but unclear documentation

Such cases require balanced review rather than default acceptance or rejection.

## **10.4 Credit Assessment Outcome**

At the end of this step, the analyst should classify credit quality as:

* Strong

* Acceptable

* Borderline

* Weak

* Unclear

---

# **11\. Step 6: Employment and Stability Review**

Employment supports the sustainability of repayment and should be reviewed separately from income amount alone.

## **11.1 Review Employment Status**

Typical employment categories include:

* Full-time salaried

* Part-time salaried

* Contract-based

* Self-employed

* Unemployed

* Other non-standard earning arrangements

## **11.2 Stability Considerations**

The analyst should consider:

* Whether the employment type implies recurring income

* Whether the profile appears stable or volatile

* Whether the employment type is consistent with declared income

* Whether additional scrutiny is needed due to non-standard status

## **11.3 Review Outcome**

Classify employment support as:

* Strongly supportive

* Generally supportive

* Needs verification

* Weakly supportive

* Not supportive

---

# **12\. Step 7: Loan Purpose and Product Fit Review**

Loan purpose affects both eligibility and risk interpretation.

## **12.1 Confirm Purpose Eligibility**

The analyst must confirm that the stated loan purpose is permitted under product rules.

Examples of common categories:

* Debt consolidation

* Medical expense

* Home-related non-mortgage expense

* Education-related expense

* General consumption

* Other disclosed personal purpose

Purposes outside consumer policy scope must be escalated or declined according to policy.

## **12.2 Interpret Risk Implications of Purpose**

Loan purpose may influence the review as follows:

* Debt consolidation: may support approval if it plausibly improves repayment burden

* Medical/emergency use: may indicate financial stress and justify additional scrutiny

* Discretionary spending: may be more sensitive in marginal profiles

* Unclear or vague purpose: may require clarification

## **12.3 Product Fit Outcome**

Classify product fit as:

* Eligible and aligned

* Eligible but higher scrutiny required

* Ambiguous

* Not eligible

---

# **13\. Step 8: Documentation Consistency Review**

A strong profile cannot be approved if key facts remain unsupported or contradictory.

## **13.1 Cross-Check Key Facts**

Analysts should compare:

* Declared income vs supporting income records

* Employment status vs supporting employment record

* Loan purpose vs provided explanation

* Risk indicators vs any case notes or prior findings

## **13.2 Common Consistency Issues**

Examples include:

* Declared income materially higher than supporting records suggest

* Employment status inconsistent with documentation

* Loan purpose changed during review without explanation

* Data fields completed but not credible in context

* Case details internally inconsistent

## **13.3 Handling Minor vs Major Inconsistencies**

### **Minor inconsistencies**

Minor issues may be clarified and resolved through routine follow-up. These do not automatically require decline.

### **Major inconsistencies**

Major inconsistencies affect the reliability of the case and may justify rejection or escalation if they cannot be resolved.

## **13.4 Documentation Review Outcome**

Classify documentation status as:

* Complete and consistent

* Adequate with minor follow-up

* Incomplete but potentially resolvable

* Materially inconsistent

* Insufficient for decisioning

---

# **14\. Step 9: Model-Supported Review**

Where a model score or probability is available, it should be used to support consistency and prioritization.

## **14.1 Permitted Uses of Model Output**

Analysts may use model outputs to:

* Understand estimated payback probability

* Identify key predictive factors

* Support queue prioritization

* Highlight cases near decision boundaries

* Compare model output with policy interpretation

## **14.2 Prohibited Uses of Model Output**

Analysts must not:

* Treat the score as a replacement for judgment

* Approve or reject solely because the model indicates a direction

* Ignore policy breaches because the model appears favorable

* Copy technical model explanations into the final note without business interpretation

## **14.3 Model-Policy Tension Review**

If the model suggests a stronger outcome than policy indicators support, the analyst should:

* Re-check affordability

* Re-check documentation

* Confirm no exclusion rule is triggered

* Consider whether the model is missing context

If policy appears favorable but model output is weak, the analyst should:

* Examine borderline variables closely

* Review explanations where available

* Verify whether weaknesses are concentrated in specific features

* Determine whether manual review can resolve uncertainty

---

# **15\. Step 10: Risk Synthesis**

After reviewing each major dimension, the analyst must synthesize the case rather than evaluating factors in isolation.

## **15.1 Build the Risk Narrative**

A complete case narrative should answer:

* Can the applicant likely afford repayment?

* Does the applicant’s credit profile support trust in repayment?

* Is the loan request proportionate to the profile?

* Is the case documented well enough to support a final decision?

* Are there compensating strengths?

* Are there unresolved concerns?

## **15.2 Weight of Evidence Principle**

Not all factors carry equal weight. Analysts should give more weight to:

* Verified affordability evidence

* Clear credit weaknesses

* Material documentation contradictions

* Critical policy triggers

Analysts should give less weight to:

* Weak contextual proxies

* Unsupported assumptions

* Non-material profile traits

* Attributes not relevant to repayment or policy fit

## **15.3 Synthesis Categories**

At this stage, the case should generally fall into one of five categories:

* Strong approval case

* Approve with caution

* Borderline review case

* Strong rejection case

* Escalation case

---

# **16\. Step 11: Decision Formulation**

The analyst must now determine the most appropriate next action.

## **16.1 Approve**

Approval is appropriate when:

* Affordability is strong or acceptable

* Credit quality is acceptable or better

* Documentation is sufficient

* Product fit is confirmed

* No critical policy issue remains

## **16.2 Review Pending Additional Information**

This outcome is appropriate when:

* A final decision depends on clarification

* The profile may be acceptable if missing facts are resolved

* Documentation gaps are important but resolvable

## **16.3 Reject**

Rejection is appropriate when:

* Affordability is weak or unverifiable

* Credit quality is materially below tolerance

* The loan request is disproportionate

* Documentation is materially inconsistent

* Product fit is not acceptable

* The analyst cannot justify repayment capacity with reasonable confidence

## **16.4 Escalate**

Escalation is appropriate when:

* The case requires override authority

* Policy interpretation is unclear

* Compensating strengths are meaningful but unusual

* The decision carries elevated risk or reputational sensitivity

---

# **17\. Step 12: Exception Review**

## **17.1 When to Consider an Exception**

An exception should only be considered if:

* The case fails a non-critical policy condition

* There is strong documented support for repayment

* The weakness is limited and explainable

* The case falls within exception governance rules

## **17.2 Required Exception Documentation**

Exception requests should include:

* Specific policy condition not met

* Why the weakness is considered manageable

* Compensating strengths

* Supporting documents reviewed

* Recommended action

* Analyst rationale

## **17.3 When Not to Consider an Exception**

Do not pursue an exception where:

* A critical exclusion rule is triggered

* Affordability is clearly inadequate

* Documentation is materially unreliable

* The rationale depends on unsupported assumptions

---

# **18\. Step 13: Escalation Guidance**

## **18.1 Cases That Must Be Escalated**

Escalation is required when:

* Analyst authority is insufficient

* Exception exceeds delegated limits

* Policy interpretation is unclear

* Multiple conflicting signals remain unresolved

* The case may create precedent or audit sensitivity

## **18.2 Escalation Package Requirements**

The analyst must provide:

* Applicant summary

* Main strengths

* Main concerns

* Key policy issue

* Decision recommendation

* Specific question for senior reviewer

Escalations must be concise and decision-oriented.

---

# **19\. Step 14: Decision Note Preparation**

Every reviewed case must include a clear and auditable decision note.

## **19.1 Required Elements**

The note must contain:

* Final disposition

* Main reasons supporting the decision

* Key risk concerns

* Any documents reviewed

* Whether clarification was requested

* Whether a model output was considered

* Whether exception or escalation was involved

## **19.2 Good Decision Note Example**

**Decision:** Review pending clarification

**Rationale:** Applicant presents stable full-time employment and income that may support the requested loan; however, debt-to-income ratio is near the upper policy tolerance and the requested amount is relatively high compared with annual income. Credit quality is borderline acceptable. Additional confirmation of current obligations is required before a final affordability determination can be made.

## **19.3 Poor Decision Note Example**

**Decision:** Reject

**Rationale:** Applicant seems risky and does not look good.

The above example is unacceptable because it is vague, unsupported, and not auditable.

---

# **20\. Step 15: Final Disposition and Logging**

Once the final action is determined:

* Update the case status

* Save the decision note

* Record any follow-up requests

* Record any exception or escalation references

* Ensure the system log reflects the final review state

The final record must allow another reviewer to understand what was reviewed, what mattered, and why the outcome was chosen.

---

# **21\. Review Heuristics by Common Pattern**

## **21.1 High Income but Borderline Credit**

This pattern may justify review rather than automatic rejection if:

* Debt burden remains manageable

* Employment is stable

* Requested amount is not excessive

* Documentation is consistent

## **21.2 Strong Credit but High Debt-to-Income Ratio**

This pattern often requires caution. The analyst should prioritize affordability over historical credit quality if current debt burden appears unsustainable.

## **21.3 Weak Documentation but Otherwise Strong Profile**

If the underlying profile appears strong, the case may remain pending for clarification rather than being immediately rejected, unless policy requires verified documentation before further progression.

## **21.4 Large Loan Request with Moderate Income**

This pattern requires careful proportionality analysis. Even acceptable credit may not offset a loan burden that appears too large for the applicant’s income.

## **21.5 Debt Consolidation Purpose with Mixed Risk**

Debt consolidation can be favorable if it plausibly reduces repayment stress, but it does not offset weak affordability or unreliable documentation.

---

# **22\. Common Analyst Mistakes**

Analysts should avoid the following:

* Approving because one strong feature dominates multiple weaknesses

* Rejecting without checking whether the case is incomplete rather than weak

* Over-relying on model output

* Ignoring documentation inconsistencies

* Failing to distinguish between borderline and prohibited cases

* Writing vague rationale notes

* Using non-policy language or subjective impressions

* Treating protected attributes as relevant risk evidence

---

# **23\. Analyst Quality Checklist**

Before finalizing a case, confirm the following:

* Have I identified the original review trigger?

* Have I reviewed affordability, credit quality, and documentation separately?

* Is the loan amount proportionate to the applicant profile?

* Is the loan purpose eligible and understood?

* Are all major facts verified or reasonably supported?

* Have I considered both strengths and weaknesses?

* Is my final rationale specific, factual, and auditable?

* If uncertain, have I escalated appropriately?

If the answer to any of the above is no, the case should not be finalized without further action.

# **26\. Glossary**

## **Case Snapshot**

A concise summary of the applicant’s financial, credit, and product profile prepared at the start of analyst review.

## **Documentation Consistency**

The degree to which declared application data aligns with supporting records and internal case information.

## **Manual Review**

A process in which a trained analyst evaluates an application that cannot be finalized through standard automated rules alone.

## **Model-Policy Tension**

A situation where the model output suggests a different direction than policy-based or documentary assessment.

## **Risk Synthesis**

The analyst’s integrated interpretation of affordability, credit quality, documentation quality, and policy fit.

---

# **27\. Final Playbook Statement**

A good manual review is structured, evidence-based, and clearly documented. The analyst’s role is to resolve uncertainty by applying underwriting policy to the specific facts of the case. Strong review practice requires balancing efficiency with rigor, using available model support appropriately, and ensuring that each final disposition is defensible, explainable, and aligned with policy.
