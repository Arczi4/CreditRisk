# **Credit Underwriting Policy Manual**

**Document ID:** CU-POL-001  
 **Version:** 1.0  
 **Effective Date:** 2026-01-01  
 **Document Owner:** Retail Credit Risk Department  
 **Classification:** Internal Use Only

---

# **1\. Purpose**

This document defines the underwriting principles, decision rules, escalation paths, and analyst review standards for unsecured consumer loan applications. It is intended to support consistent, explainable, and policy-aligned credit decisions across automated and manual review workflows.

This policy applies to all retail installment loan applications processed through the bank’s consumer lending platform, including applications initially assessed by scoring models and subsequently reviewed by a credit analyst.

---

# **2\. Scope**

This policy covers:

* New unsecured personal loan applications

* Loan refinancing requests treated as new credit applications

* Applications routed to manual review due to risk, incompleteness, or policy exceptions

* Analyst actions related to approval, rejection, escalation, and conditional approval

This policy does not cover:

* Mortgage lending

* Secured auto lending

* Small business lending

* Collections, restructurings, or hardship programs

* Fraud investigation procedures beyond initial underwriting red flags

---

# **3\. Underwriting Objectives**

The bank’s underwriting process is designed to achieve the following objectives:

1. Approve applicants with demonstrated capacity and willingness to repay

2. Decline applicants whose risk exceeds the bank’s tolerance

3. Route borderline or exceptional cases to trained analysts for further review

4. Ensure decisions are explainable, consistent, and auditable

5. Prevent over-indebtedness and inappropriate credit extension

6. Comply with internal risk standards and fair lending principles

Underwriting decisions must balance portfolio growth, credit quality, customer treatment, and operational consistency.

---

# **4\. Core Underwriting Principles**

## **4.1 Repayment Capacity**

A loan may only be approved where there is reasonable evidence that the applicant can sustain repayment over the full loan term without undue financial stress.

Indicators of repayment capacity include:

* Stable and sufficient annual income

* Manageable debt-to-income ratio

* Loan amount proportionate to income profile

* Consistent employment or income-generating activity

* No material contradictions in financial disclosures

## **4.2 Credit Willingness**

An applicant’s prior repayment behavior is a key indicator of likely future performance.

Indicators of positive credit willingness include:

* Strong or acceptable credit score

* Limited adverse repayment history

* No recent serious delinquencies

* No unresolved default markers, where applicable

* Credit profile consistent with responsible borrowing behavior

## **4.3 Proportionality**

Loan size, pricing, and term must be proportionate to the applicant’s risk and financial profile. High requested amounts combined with weak income or marginal credit quality require enhanced scrutiny.

## **4.4 Explainability**

Every final decision must be explainable in business terms. Analysts must be able to identify the principal approval, review, or decline drivers using applicant data, documented policy standards, and model-supported evidence where available.

## **4.5 Human Judgment in Borderline Cases**

Automated risk outputs support decision-making but do not replace analyst judgment in manually reviewed applications. Analysts are expected to evaluate the full case, including contextual and documentary evidence, when an application falls near decision boundaries or presents compensating factors.

# **5\. Application Data Inputs**

The following applicant attributes are generally considered during underwriting:

* Annual income

* Debt-to-income ratio

* Credit score

* Requested loan amount

* Interest rate

* Gender

* Marital status

* Education level

* Employment status

* Loan purpose

* Grade/subgrade or internal risk tier

Additional supporting data may include income documents, employment evidence, internal policy flags, and analyst notes.

## **5.1 Sensitive and Protected Attributes**

Sensitive or potentially protected attributes must not be used as the sole basis for adverse treatment. Where such attributes are present in source systems, their use must comply with internal governance and applicable risk control standards.

Analysts must not justify a decline using protected class language. Final case notes must focus on objective risk and policy-based factors such as affordability, credit history, documentation sufficiency, or policy limits.

---

# **6\. Decision Categories**

All applications must result in one of the following decisions:

## **6.1 Approve**

An application may be approved when:

* The applicant demonstrates sufficient repayment capacity

* Credit profile is within acceptable tolerance

* No material documentation gaps remain

* No critical policy rule is breached

* No unresolved red flag requires additional review

## **6.2 Review**

An application must be placed into manual review when:

* Risk indicators are mixed or borderline

* The automated score is inconclusive relative to policy thresholds

* Documentation is incomplete or inconsistent

* A potential exception or compensating factor exists

* Analyst clarification is needed before a final decision

## **6.3 Reject**

An application must be rejected when:

* Risk materially exceeds tolerance

* Repayment capacity is insufficient

* Required documentation cannot support the stated profile

* Critical policy exclusion criteria are triggered

* The analyst cannot reasonably justify affordability or credit acceptability

---

# **7\. Approval Framework**

## **7.1 Standard Approval Conditions**

A standard approval generally requires all of the following:

* Credit score at or above the bank’s minimum acceptable threshold for the relevant product tier

* Debt-to-income ratio within the acceptable range

* Annual income sufficient relative to requested loan amount and repayment burden

* Employment status that supports recurring repayment capacity

* No major unresolved inconsistencies in the application

* Loan purpose eligible under product policy

* Risk grade within approvable range or supported by compensating strengths

## **7.2 Compensating Strengths**

An application with one moderate weakness may still be approved if supported by multiple strengths, such as:

* High and stable income

* Strong credit score

* Low leverage outside the requested loan

* Conservative requested loan amount

* Long employment tenure or stable professional profile

* Clear and consistent supporting documentation

Compensating strengths must not be used to override a critical exclusion criterion.

---

# **8\. Review Triggers**

Applications should be routed to manual review when one or more of the following conditions apply.

## **8.1 Affordability Borderline**

Manual review is required when debt servicing capacity appears borderline, such as:

* Debt-to-income ratio near the bank’s upper tolerance

* Requested loan amount large relative to annual income

* Moderate income with elevated pricing or repayment burden

* Material discrepancy between stated income and expected affordability

## **8.2 Credit Profile Borderline**

Manual review is required when:

* Credit score is close to the minimum acceptable range

* Credit profile contains mixed signals

* Recent repayment behavior is weaker than historical profile

* Risk grade is marginal but not automatically disqualifying

## **8.3 Documentation Concerns**

Manual review is required when:

* Income evidence is incomplete

* Declared employment status is not fully supported

* Application fields conflict with uploaded documents

* Supporting records are legible but ambiguous

* Clarification may resolve uncertainty

## **8.4 Policy Ambiguity**

Manual review is required when:

* Loan purpose may fit an exception category

* Product eligibility is unclear

* The applicant profile does not fit standard policy buckets

* A policy interpretation issue exists

## **8.5 Model-Policy Tension**

Manual review is required when:

* The model output and policy indicators are not aligned

* The score suggests acceptance but a policy concern exists

* The score suggests elevated risk but the applicant presents meaningful compensating strengths

---

# **9\. Reject Triggers**

The following generally require rejection unless a separately approved exception pathway exists.

## **9.1 Insufficient Repayment Capacity**

Reject when:

* Verified or reasonably inferred income is insufficient for the requested obligation

* Debt-to-income ratio materially exceeds acceptable levels

* Loan burden is disproportionate to stated financial profile

* The applicant’s financial position indicates likely unsustainable repayment

## **9.2 Unacceptable Credit Risk**

Reject when:

* Credit score is materially below the bank’s minimum tolerance

* Risk grade falls into a non-approvable tier

* The overall risk profile indicates high probability of non-repayment

* Adverse repayment indicators outweigh all positive factors

## **9.3 Ineligible Loan Purpose**

Reject when the requested purpose is not supported under consumer lending policy or falls outside product scope.

## **9.4 Unsupported or Contradictory Information**

Reject when:

* Material application data cannot be verified

* Supporting documents materially contradict declared information

* The analyst cannot establish a reliable financial profile

* Core case facts remain unresolved after reasonable follow-up

## **9.5 Critical Policy Breach**

Reject when any mandatory exclusion criterion is triggered, including:

* Non-permitted product-purpose combination

* Risk grade outside policy limits

* Insufficient documentation after final follow-up

* Clear affordability failure

* Other explicitly non-overridable rule defined by product governance

---

# **10\. Risk Assessment Dimensions**

Analysts must evaluate the application across the following dimensions.

## **10.1 Annual Income**

Annual income should be assessed for:

* Sufficiency relative to requested amount

* Stability and plausibility

* Consistency with employment status

* Alignment with supporting documentation

Higher income generally supports stronger repayment capacity, but only if stable and credible.

## **10.2 Debt-to-Income Ratio**

Debt-to-income ratio is a primary affordability measure.

General interpretation:

* Low ratio: favorable

* Moderate ratio: acceptable with supporting strengths

* Elevated ratio: caution

* Very high ratio: strong decline indicator

Debt-to-income ratio should not be interpreted in isolation. The analyst must consider income stability, requested amount, and pricing.

## **10.3 Credit Score**

Credit score is a central measure of historical repayment reliability.

General interpretation:

* Strong score: positive indicator

* Moderate score: acceptable with context

* Borderline score: manual review

* Weak score: decline unless specific exception framework applies

## **10.4 Requested Loan Amount**

Loan amount must be proportionate to:

* Annual income

* Existing obligations

* Credit profile

* Product purpose

* Risk grade

A large requested amount may remain approvable for a strong applicant, but requires scrutiny when paired with affordability pressure or weak credit signals.

## **10.5 Interest Rate**

Interest rate can signal pricing for risk and affects repayment burden. Elevated interest rates increase monthly obligation and may worsen affordability. Analysts should consider whether the offered pricing remains sustainable for the applicant profile.

## **10.6 Employment Status**

Employment status must support repayment continuity.

General interpretation:

* Full-time salaried: generally favorable

* Long-term contract: acceptable with verification

* Self-employed: review required unless well-documented

* Part-time or unstable employment: elevated scrutiny

* Unemployed: generally non-approvable absent specific alternate income framework

## **10.7 Education Level**

Education level may provide context but must not be treated as a sole decision driver. It may be considered only as a supporting profile attribute and never as a substitute for affordability or credit analysis.

## **10.8 Marital Status and Gender**

These attributes must not determine the lending outcome. Analysts must not document adverse action rationales using these attributes. Any use must be consistent with internal governance and fairness controls.

## **10.9 Loan Purpose**

Loan purpose affects both eligibility and risk interpretation.

Examples:

* Debt consolidation: may be favorable if it improves repayment profile and the case is otherwise sound

* Medical or emergency needs: may require caution due to stress context

* Discretionary consumption: may warrant stricter scrutiny in weaker profiles

* Business-related use: may be outside consumer policy scope depending on product rules

## **10.10 Grade/Subgrade or Internal Risk Tier**

Risk tier summarizes overall risk quality and may incorporate bureau, affordability, and internal scoring indicators. Lower-quality grades require more scrutiny and may be non-approvable at the most adverse levels.

# **11\. Decisioning Guidance by Combined Risk Pattern**

## **11.1 Strong Approval Pattern**

Applications are generally approvable when most of the following are present:

* Strong or acceptable credit score

* Low to moderate debt-to-income ratio

* Stable employment

* Income comfortably supports the loan

* Requested amount proportionate to profile

* Eligible loan purpose

* No major data inconsistencies

## **11.2 Borderline Review Pattern**

Applications should be reviewed when the profile shows a mix such as:

* Acceptable income but marginal credit score

* Strong credit score but elevated debt-to-income ratio

* Good affordability but weak or unclear documentation

* Moderate risk grade with strong compensating factors

* Large loan amount despite otherwise acceptable profile

## **11.3 Decline Pattern**

Applications are generally rejectable when multiple adverse factors align, such as:

* Low credit score

* High debt-to-income ratio

* Weak employment stability

* Requested amount disproportionate to income

* Ineligible purpose or unsupported application data

* Adverse risk grade with no meaningful compensating strengths

---

# **12\. Documentation Requirements**

The analyst must confirm that the case file contains sufficient evidence to support the final decision.

Typical required documentation may include:

* Proof of identity

* Proof of income

* Employment verification

* Supporting explanation for stated loan purpose, where required

* Additional clarification documents in exception or borderline cases

A decision must not rely on unverified assumptions where supporting documentation is required by policy.

---

# **13\. Analyst Review Standards**

## **13.1 Required Review Approach**

For manually reviewed applications, the analyst must:

1. Review core applicant data

2. Assess affordability

3. Assess credit quality

4. Verify consistency across the application and documents

5. Determine whether policy rules are met

6. Identify compensating factors and risk concerns

7. Record a concise, evidence-based rationale

## **13.2 Decision Rationale Standards**

Each analyst decision note must include:

* Final decision

* Main supporting or adverse factors

* Whether additional documents were reviewed

* Whether any exception was considered

* Why the final decision is consistent with policy

Decision notes must be factual, concise, and suitable for audit.

## **13.3 Prohibited Analyst Practices**

Analysts must not:

* Approve based on intuition alone

* Ignore material policy breaches

* Use protected characteristics as a decision basis

* Copy unsupported model output as a substitute for judgment

* Record vague justifications such as “seems risky” without evidence

---

# **14\. Use of Model Outputs**

Automated model outputs may be used to support prioritization and consistency, including:

* Payback probability estimation

* Risk ranking

* Explanation of primary predictive factors

* Routing to approve/review/reject queues

However:

* Model output does not override policy

* Model output does not replace documentation review

* Analyst judgment is required for review cases and exceptions

* Any final decision must remain explainable in business terms

Where model explanations are available, they should be used as supporting evidence rather than as sole decision rationale.

---

# **15\. Exception Handling**

A case may be considered for exception review only when:

* The case fails a non-critical policy condition

* There is strong documented evidence of compensating strength

* The analyst can clearly justify why risk remains acceptable

* The exception falls within an authorized override framework

Exceptions require:

* Clear rationale

* Supporting documents

* Approval by the designated authority level

* Recording in the audit trail

Critical exclusion rules are not eligible for exception unless explicitly stated in separate override policy.

---

# **16\. Escalation Rules**

A case must be escalated when:

* The applicant profile is materially outside standard policy but not clearly rejectable

* A requested exception exceeds analyst authority

* There is conflict between policy interpretation and system outcome

* Material concerns remain unresolved after standard review

* Senior review is required for reputational or risk reasons

Escalated cases must include a concise summary of:

* Key facts

* Main risks

* Compensating strengths

* Requested decision or guidance

---

# **17\. Quality Assurance and Auditability**

All decisions must be auditable. The following records must be retained in the case file:

* Application data used in decisioning

* Relevant documents reviewed

* Model output, if applicable

* Decision category

* Analyst note

* Exception or escalation record, if any

* Timestamp and analyst identifier

Quality assurance teams may periodically review decisions for:

* Policy adherence

* Consistency

* Documentation quality

* Appropriate use of manual judgment

* Fair treatment and rationale quality

---

# **18\. Fair Lending and Responsible Decisioning**

The bank is committed to consistent and fair decisioning. Analysts must ensure that:

* Decisions are based on objective, policy-relevant risk factors

* Similar cases are treated consistently

* Explanations are grounded in financial and policy logic

* Sensitive attributes are not used as stand-alone reasons for adverse treatment

* Borderline cases receive balanced and evidence-based review

Potential fairness concerns must be escalated to the appropriate governance function.

---

# **19\. Example Decision Rationales**

## **19.1 Approval Example**

**Decision:** Approve

**Rationale:** Applicant demonstrates strong repayment capacity based on verified income, moderate debt-to-income ratio, and stable full-time employment. Credit score is within the acceptable range and requested loan amount is proportionate to the applicant’s profile. No material documentation gaps or policy breaches identified.

## **19.2 Review Example**

**Decision:** Review

**Rationale:** Applicant shows acceptable income and stable employment, but debt-to-income ratio is near the upper tolerance and requested loan amount is elevated relative to annual income. Credit score is borderline acceptable. Additional confirmation of current obligations is required before final decision.

## **19.3 Reject Example**

**Decision:** Reject

**Rationale:** Applicant presents insufficient repayment capacity due to elevated debt-to-income ratio and income level not adequate for the requested obligation. Credit profile is below the acceptable risk threshold and no compensating strengths sufficient to offset the risk were identified. Decision is consistent with affordability and risk policy.

---

# **20\. Policy Interpretation Notes**

When this policy does not explicitly address a case detail, analysts must apply the following order of interpretation:

1. Product eligibility rules

2. Critical exclusion criteria

3. Affordability assessment

4. Credit quality assessment

5. Documentation sufficiency

6. Compensating strengths and exception framework

7. Escalation to a senior authority if ambiguity remains

Where ambiguity exists, the more conservative interpretation should be applied until guidance is obtained.

---

# **21\. Review and Maintenance**

This policy must be reviewed at least annually or earlier if triggered by:

* Material changes in credit strategy

* Model changes affecting underwriting workflow

* Regulatory or governance updates

* Portfolio performance deterioration

* Internal audit findings

Changes must be version-controlled and approved by the Retail Credit Risk Department.

---

# **22\. Glossary**

## **Affordability**

The applicant’s ability to sustain loan repayment given income, obligations, and pricing.

## **Compensating Strength**

A favorable aspect of the case that may partially offset a moderate weakness, without overriding a critical policy breach.

## **Debt-to-Income Ratio**

A measure of debt burden relative to income, used to assess repayment capacity.

## **Exception**

A policy deviation considered within an approved override framework.

## **Manual Review**

A case status requiring analyst evaluation before final decision.

## **Risk Grade/Subgrade**

A risk classification used to summarize or support overall borrower quality assessment.

## **Payback Probability**

A model-estimated likelihood that the applicant will repay the loan according to contractual expectations.

---

# **23\. Final Policy Statement**

Approval decisions must be based on a balanced evaluation of affordability, credit quality, documentation sufficiency, and policy fit. Automated model outputs may support efficiency and consistency, but the final underwriting rationale must remain grounded in objective financial evidence and documented policy standards.

Where risk is unclear, analysts are expected to review carefully, document clearly, and escalate appropriately.
