# **Adverse Decision and Review Communication Guide**

**Document ID:** AD-COM-005  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Effective Date:** 2026-01-01  
**Document Owner:** Retail Credit Operations and Customer Communications  
**Classification:** Internal Use Only

---

# **1\. Purpose**

This guide defines the communication standards for adverse lending decisions, pending review outcomes, requests for additional information, and internal case disposition summaries in the unsecured consumer loan underwriting process.

Its purpose is to ensure that all communication related to loan decisioning is:

* clear  
* consistent  
* accurate  
* respectful  
* auditable  
* aligned with underwriting policy and internal governance standards

This guide applies to both analyst-authored communications and AI-assisted draft communications used in internal workflows.

---

# **2\. Scope**

This guide applies to:

* Internal analyst case notes  
* Internal review summaries  
* Requests for additional information  
* Pending review updates  
* Rejection rationale summaries  
* Adverse decision explanation drafts  
* Escalation notes  
* AI-assisted communication suggestions for analyst review

This guide does not replace legal notification requirements, regulatory adverse action obligations, or jurisdiction-specific communication rules. Where formal compliance language is required, the approved compliance template takes precedence.

---

# **3\. Communication Objectives**

The objectives of adverse and review communication are to:

1. Explain the case status clearly  
2. Use standardized business language  
3. Reflect the actual decision rationale accurately  
4. Avoid subjective or emotionally loaded wording  
5. Ensure communications remain grounded in documented facts  
6. Protect the bank from inconsistent or unsupported explanations  
7. Help analysts produce high-quality, reviewable summaries efficiently

---

# **4\. Core Communication Principles**

## **4.1 Be Clear**

Communication must be understandable to the intended audience. Internal notes should be concise and specific. Customer-facing drafts should avoid technical jargon where possible.

## **4.2 Be Accurate**

Every communication must reflect the actual case facts, review findings, and policy-based rationale. Analysts must not include language that overstates confidence or introduces unsupported assumptions.

## **4.3 Be Respectful**

All communications must be professional and neutral in tone. Rejection or review language must not imply blame, carelessness, or personal judgment.

## **4.4 Be Specific but Controlled**

Communications should identify the relevant issue category, such as affordability, income support, or documentation sufficiency, without exposing unnecessary internal details or model internals.

## **4.5 Be Consistent**

Similar cases should be described using similar language patterns. This promotes fairness, auditability, and better quality control.

## **4.6 Do Not Use Protected Characteristic Language**

Communications must never cite gender, marital status, education level, or any protected or sensitive characteristic as a reason for review or rejection.

# **6\. Audience Types**

## **6.1 Internal Analyst and Reviewer Audience**

Internal communication may include more operational detail, including:

* affordability findings  
* documentation inconsistencies  
* escalation logic  
* model-supported reasoning summaries  
* policy interpretation notes

However, internal notes must still remain professional, factual, and auditable.

## **6.2 Customer-Facing Audience**

Customer-facing communication must be:

* simpler  
* less technical  
* non-judgmental  
* compliant with approved wording standards  
* limited to appropriate business reasons

Customer-facing language must not disclose internal model mechanics, internal scoring methodology, or unnecessary operational process details.

---

# **7\. Standard Tone Requirements**

All communications governed by this guide must use the following tone characteristics:

* neutral  
* professional  
* factual  
* concise  
* respectful  
* non-accusatory

The following tone styles are prohibited:

* dismissive  
* sarcastic  
* speculative  
* overly casual  
* emotionally charged  
* punitive  
* vague and evasive

---

# **8\. General Writing Rules**

## **8.1 State the Outcome Clearly**

The communication should make the case status immediately clear, such as:

* approved  
* under review  
* additional information required  
* declined  
* escalated

## **8.2 Explain the Main Reason in Plain Business Language**

The communication should identify the principal reason category using language such as:

* income could not be sufficiently supported  
* current debt obligations appear high relative to income  
* additional documentation is needed  
* the requested amount is not proportionate to the current financial profile

## **8.3 Avoid Unnecessary Technical Language**

Do not use internal technical wording such as:

* negative coefficient contribution  
* low model confidence interval  
* threshold breach on transformed feature  
* score banding logic triggered

Instead, translate technical logic into business-relevant explanation.

## **8.4 Do Not Over-Explain**

Communications should provide enough information to be understandable, but should not include excessive internal commentary, all possible contributing factors, or speculative narrative.

## **8.5 Avoid Absolute Statements Unless Supported**

Avoid phrases such as:

* applicant cannot repay  
* applicant is financially irresponsible  
* applicant is high risk in all circumstances

Use controlled wording such as:

* the current profile does not provide sufficient confidence in repayment capacity  
* available information does not support approval under current policy standards

# **10\. Internal Analyst Disposition Notes**

## **10.1 Purpose**

Internal disposition notes explain the final or interim analyst conclusion in a way that another reviewer, manager, auditor, or quality assurance reviewer can understand.

## **10.2 Required Elements**

An internal disposition note should include:

* case outcome  
* main supporting or adverse factors  
* whether documents were reviewed  
* whether clarification was requested  
* whether the decision is final or pending  
* whether escalation or exception review was considered

## **10.3 Standard Structure**

Recommended structure:

1. Outcome  
2. Main rationale  
3. Supporting facts  
4. Follow-up or escalation note if applicable

## **10.4 Example — Approval**

**Outcome:** Approve

**Rationale:** Applicant demonstrates sufficient repayment capacity based on verified income, stable employment, and debt burden within acceptable tolerance. Credit profile is supportive, requested amount is proportionate to verified income, and no material documentation inconsistencies were identified.

## **10.5 Example — Review Pending**

**Outcome:** Review pending clarification

**Rationale:** Applicant presents generally supportive income and employment indicators; however, current debt burden appears near the upper tolerance and the requested amount is relatively high compared with verified income. Additional confirmation of existing obligations is required before final affordability can be confirmed.

## **10.6 Example — Reject**

**Outcome:** Reject

**Rationale:** Verified income does not sufficiently support the requested loan amount, debt burden appears elevated relative to earnings, and the credit profile is below the bank’s acceptable range. The overall case does not provide sufficient confidence in sustainable repayment capacity.

# **11\. Internal Review Summaries**

## **11.1 Purpose**

An internal review summary is used when a case is handed off, quality-checked, escalated, or reviewed by another analyst.

## **11.2 Required Content**

The summary should contain:

* applicant profile snapshot  
* key risk concerns  
* key supporting strengths  
* outstanding issues  
* analyst recommendation

## **11.3 Example Template**

**Case Summary:** Applicant reports stable employment and moderate annual income. Requested loan amount is elevated relative to income. Debt-to-income ratio is near policy tolerance. Credit profile is borderline acceptable. Documents generally support employment and income, but further clarification is needed on current obligations. Recommendation: hold for clarification prior to final affordability decision.

## **11.4 Writing Rule**

Internal review summaries should not repeat every data point. They should focus on what matters to the next decision-maker.

---

# **12\. Requests for Additional Information**

## **12.1 Purpose**

Requests for additional information are used when the application cannot be finalized because one or more material facts remain unclear, incomplete, or unsupported.

## **12.2 Communication Principles**

A request for information should:

* clearly state what is needed  
* briefly explain why it is needed  
* avoid accusatory tone  
* avoid suggesting the case will definitely be approved once the information is received  
* avoid unnecessary detail

## **12.3 Approved Template Style**

**Status:** Additional information required

**Message:** We require additional information to continue reviewing your application. At this stage, we are unable to complete the assessment because certain income or financial details could not yet be fully confirmed. Please provide the requested supporting information so that the review can continue.

## **12.4 Internal Analyst Version**

**Rationale:** Current file does not provide sufficient support for final affordability assessment. Additional evidence is required to confirm income stability and existing repayment obligations.

## **12.5 Prohibited Wording**

Do not write:

* We think you may have provided incorrect information  
* Your documents are suspicious  
* Your income does not look real

Unless a specific fraud escalation procedure applies, neutral language must be used.

---

# **13\. Review Pending Communication**

## **13.1 Purpose**

Review pending communication is used when the case remains open for analyst assessment, follow-up, or senior review.

## **13.2 Recommended Messaging**

The communication should make clear that:

* the application is still under review  
* additional assessment is required  
* no final decision has yet been made

## **13.3 Example — Customer-Safe Draft**

**Status:** Application under review

**Message:** Your application is currently under review. Additional assessment is required to complete the evaluation of the information provided. We will continue processing the application in line with our standard review procedures.

## **13.4 Example — Internal Note**

**Status:** Pending analyst review

**Rationale:** Case remains open due to mixed affordability and credit signals. Documentation is mostly supportive, but further review is required before a final recommendation can be issued.

---

# **14\. Internal Rejection Rationale Summaries**

## **14.1 Purpose**

Internal rejection rationale summaries are used to document the substantive basis for decline in business language suitable for audit and supervisory review.

## **14.2 Minimum Requirements**

A rejection summary should include:

* the main decline driver  
* any secondary supporting concerns  
* confirmation that the rationale is policy-consistent  
* a clear explanation of why the case is not approvable

## **14.3 Standard Structure**

Recommended structure:

1. Primary decline reason  
2. Secondary contributing factors  
3. Policy fit statement

## **14.4 Example — Affordability-Led Decline**

**Decision:** Reject

**Rationale:** The primary decline driver is insufficient repayment capacity. Verified income does not adequately support the requested loan amount, and current debt burden appears elevated relative to earnings. Credit quality does not provide sufficient compensating strength to offset the affordability concern. Outcome is consistent with affordability and underwriting policy standards.

## **14.5 Example — Documentation-Led Decline**

**Decision:** Reject

**Rationale:** The application could not be supported to approval because declared income and employment details were not sufficiently supported by the documentation provided. Material inconsistencies remain unresolved, and the case file does not provide a reliable basis for final underwriting confidence.

# **15\. Customer-Safe Adverse Explanation Drafts**

## **15.1 Purpose**

Customer-safe adverse explanation drafts are simplified explanations intended for controlled external communication or draft preparation. These drafts must be reviewed and aligned with approved compliance standards before use.

## **15.2 Content Standards**

The explanation should:

* remain polite and neutral  
* use business-friendly wording  
* focus on broad reason categories  
* avoid internal score terms  
* avoid legal overreach  
* avoid protected or sensitive factors

## **15.3 Example — Affordability**

We are unable to approve the application based on the current information available regarding income, existing financial obligations, and the requested credit amount.

## **15.4 Example — Documentation**

We were unable to complete the application positively because the information provided did not sufficiently confirm the financial details needed for assessment.

## **15.5 Example — Credit Profile**

We are unable to approve the application because the current credit profile does not meet the lending requirements for this product.

## **15.6 Combined Example**

We are unable to approve the application based on the current assessment of income, existing financial obligations, and overall credit profile.

## **15.7 Important Limitation**

These examples are drafting aids only. Final customer-facing wording must follow approved legal and compliance standards where required.

---

# **16\. Escalation Communication**

## **16.1 Purpose**

Escalation communication is used when a case must be referred to a senior analyst, exception authority, or specialist review queue.

## **16.2 Required Elements**

Escalation notes should include:

* why the case is being escalated  
* the key issue requiring review  
* the analyst’s recommendation  
* a concise summary of strengths and risks

## **16.3 Example**

**Escalation Reason:** Potential exception review

**Summary:** Applicant presents strong verified income and stable employment, but debt burden is slightly above standard tolerance and risk grade is marginal. Documentation is otherwise supportive. Recommending senior review to determine whether compensating strengths are sufficient for exception consideration.

## **16.4 Tone Requirement**

Escalation notes should be decision-oriented, not narrative-heavy. The goal is to help the next reviewer act efficiently.

---

# **17\. Exception Review Communication**

## **17.1 Purpose**

Exception review communication is used when a case is not clearly approvable under standard rules but may be considered under an authorized exception pathway.

## **17.2 Required Elements**

An exception summary should include:

* the policy condition not met  
* why the case may still be supportable  
* which strengths are compensating  
* what approval is being requested

## **17.3 Example**

**Exception Summary:** The application does not fully meet the standard affordability tolerance due to moderately elevated debt burden. However, the applicant demonstrates strong verified income, stable long-term employment, and otherwise supportive credit history. Documentation is complete and consistent. Requesting exception review based on compensating financial strengths and overall case stability.

## **17.4 Prohibited Language**

Do not write:

* I think we should just approve this one  
* This rule can probably be ignored  
* Customer seems trustworthy despite the numbers

Exception requests must remain evidence-based and policy-aware.

# **18\. AI-Assisted Communication Drafting Standards**

## **18.1 Permitted Use**

AI-assisted drafting tools may be used to:

* summarize review findings  
* convert reason codes into business language  
* draft internal case notes  
* propose request-for-information wording  
* generate customer-safe explanation drafts for review

## **18.2 Required Human Review**

All AI-generated communication must be reviewed by a qualified analyst before use. The analyst remains responsible for:

* factual accuracy  
* policy alignment  
* tone appropriateness  
* removal of unsupported claims  
* protection against prohibited wording

## **18.3 AI Output Restrictions**

AI-generated drafts must not:

* invent missing facts  
* cite protected characteristics  
* reveal internal model mechanics  
* state legal conclusions without approved templates  
* imply certainty not supported by the case

# **19\. Standard Language Patterns**

The following patterns are approved for general use.

## **19.1 Approved Introductory Phrases**

* Based on the current information available  
* Following review of the application and supporting information  
* The current file does not provide sufficient support for  
* Additional information is required to complete the assessment  
* The application requires further review before a final decision can be made

## **19.2 Approved Risk Explanation Phrases**

* repayment capacity could not be sufficiently supported  
* existing financial obligations appear high relative to income  
* the requested amount is not proportionate to the current financial profile  
* the current credit profile does not meet lending requirements  
* the documentation provided does not sufficiently confirm the declared information  
* the case requires additional review due to mixed indicators

## **19.3 Approved Closing Phrases**

* outcome is consistent with current underwriting standards  
* further review is required before the application can progress  
* additional documentation is needed to continue the assessment  
* the current information does not support approval under standard lending criteria

---

# **20\. Prohibited Language and Style Patterns**

The following language must not be used in internal or external communication unless specifically required by a separate approved process.

## **20.1 Subjective Judgments**

Do not use:

* applicant seems unreliable  
* customer looks risky  
* this profile feels weak  
* case does not look good

## **20.2 Blame-Oriented Language**

Do not use:

* applicant failed to prove  
* customer gave bad documents  
* applicant caused the decline  
* they should have known better

## **20.3 Unsupported Certainty**

Do not use:

* applicant will default  
* applicant cannot repay  
* this loan would definitely fail  
* the customer is financially unstable in general

## **20.4 Internal Technical Language**

Do not use:

* score threshold failure on transformed variables  
* coefficient contribution was negative  
* model confidence was low  
* adverse outcome due to feature-weight interaction

## **20.5 Sensitive Attribute Language**

Do not use:

* due to marital status  
* due to gender  
* based on family profile  
* because of education background

# **21\. Communication by Common Case Pattern**

## **21.1 Strong Profile but Missing Documents**

Recommended wording:  
The application shows several supportive indicators; however, additional documentation is required before a final decision can be completed.

## **21.2 Borderline Credit with Acceptable Income**

Recommended wording:  
The application requires additional review due to a credit profile that is near the bank’s standard approval range, despite otherwise supportive income information.

## **21.3 High Debt Burden with Moderate Income**

Recommended wording:  
The current assessment indicates that existing financial obligations are high relative to available income, which limits confidence in sustainable repayment capacity.

## **21.4 Inconsistent Information Across Documents**

Recommended wording:  
The information provided could not be fully reconciled across the application and supporting records, and the file does not currently support a final positive decision.

## **21.5 Senior Review Needed**

Recommended wording:  
The application requires additional internal review before a final decision can be issued.

# **22\. Internal-to-External Translation Guidance**

In some workflows, an internal analyst explanation must be translated into a customer-safe draft. The translation should:

* preserve the core reason  
* remove internal operational language  
* avoid model terminology  
* simplify wording  
* retain neutrality

## **22.1 Example**

**Internal:**  
Verified income does not adequately support the requested amount, debt-to-income ratio is above preferred tolerance, and current credit profile does not provide sufficient compensating strength.

**Customer-Safe Draft:**  
We are unable to approve the application based on the current assessment of income, existing financial obligations, and overall credit profile.

---

# **23\. Quality Checklist for Communication**

Before finalizing a communication, confirm:

* Is the decision status clear?  
* Is the explanation factually correct?  
* Is the wording consistent with policy?  
* Is the tone neutral and respectful?  
* Is the reason explained in business language?  
* Does the message avoid protected or sensitive attributes?  
* Does the communication avoid internal technical terminology?  
* Would another reviewer understand and accept the explanation?

If the answer to any of the above is no, the communication should be revised before use.

# **27\. Glossary**

## **Adverse Decision**

A decision resulting in decline, non-approval, or another unfavorable outcome for the application.

## **Customer-Safe Draft**

A simplified explanation format intended for controlled external use or for preparation of customer-facing communication.

## **Internal Disposition Note**

A case note written for internal review, audit, or operational workflow purposes.

## **Pending Review**

A case status indicating that a final decision has not yet been made and additional assessment is required.

## **Reason Code**

A standardized label used internally to describe a decision-relevant factor in consistent business language.

## **Escalation Note**

A concise internal summary used when a case requires review by a more senior decision-maker or specialist team.

---

# **28\. Final Guide Statement**

Good communication in underwriting is clear, factual, and controlled. It explains the outcome without introducing unsupported judgment, unnecessary technical detail, or inconsistent wording. Whether the application is approved, held for review, or declined, the communication must reflect the actual substance of the case and remain suitable for operational review, audit, and responsible customer treatment.

Analysts are expected to use this guide to produce consistent, respectful, and policy-aligned communication that supports both sound decisioning and sound documentation.
